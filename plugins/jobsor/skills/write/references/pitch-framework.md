# The researched pitch (founder or hiring manager, cold)

From the JOBSOR website (`docs/PITCH_FRAMEWORK.md`, owner's rules 4 to 7 Nov 2026). The goal: a stranger, with no meeting and nobody to
name, reads it, can tell the user did their homework, sees one real thing the user has done that fits, and replies.

## Research first (Exa and Firecrawl)
1. The person's own recent posts, talks, articles and podcasts: search `"<full name>" <company>` with Exa; LinkedIn posts through Exa's
   copy (`site:linkedin.com/posts "<full name>"`), never by opening linkedin.com. Keep only items from the last 90 days that are clearly
   theirs (their name with this company, or their own profile link). Never mix people up.
2. Company news from the last 90 days: launches, funding, hiring, new markets.
3. **Pick the opening: newest first.** Among the 3 newest good items, pick the one closest to the job. Note it in the Messages row with its
   date and link, but use it only if it is relevant to the job or the user's work. If nothing relevant is found, the message has no
   opening about them (purpose first line only) and Notes say so.
4. Warnings to tell the user before writing: comments or a post say the role is closed; a post says the person left or joined another
   company. Then stop for that person.

## The message, in order
1. **Greeting:** "Hi {first_name},". To a team inbox: "Hi {company} team,".
2. **Purpose, first line:** if this person posted the job: "I saw your post about the {role} role at {company}." Otherwise: "I'd like to
   apply for the {role} role at {company}: {job link}". If a post or news item of theirs from the last 90 days is **relevant to the job or
   to the user's work**, add one sentence about it in its own words with its date ("Your post on 12 Nov about cutting sign-up steps..."). If
   nothing relevant was found, skip it: never open with something that only repeats what they already know.
3. **Connecting line (optional, one sentence):** what they build or what the role does, in the post's or company's own words, then that it
   is close to the user's work, only when a confirmed fact shows it ("Acme builds billing APIs for clinics, and APIs for payments are what I
   built at Beta."). No guesses about their problems.
4. **If the post asks for something specific** (a note on a system you built, your hardest problem, a portfolio piece): answer that
   directly, from one confirmed fact, and say so ("You asked for a note on a system I've owned, so here's mine.").
5. **Why I fit: 2 to 4 short bullets**, each one confirmed fact matched to one need the job states, the need in the job's own words, the
   fact with its numbers as written in the profile. Recent work first. A project is used exactly as the fact says.
6. **Gap line (only if needed), after the bullets:** the profile's gap line, and if a confirmed fact allows, an offer such as "happy to show
   this in a technical round".
7. **"My resume is attached."**
8. **Ask:** "If this aligns with you, can we have a quick chat?"
9. **Signature:** the user's sign-off, name, phone (only if the profile has one), LinkedIn link from the profile.

About 120 to 180 words. One resume per company. Subject: "{role} at {company} – {user name}" (add the city when the post names one).

### Example shape (invented people and companies)
Hi Priya,

I saw your post about the Data Analyst role at Brightpay. Your note that "most users quit at the PAN step" is the kind of drop-off I've
worked on.

Why I think I fit:
- Funnel analysis: at Beta (Jan to Jun 2026) I found half of new users stopped at the address step; cutting it to one field took
  activation from 31% to 44%.
- SQL and dashboards: I built the weekly activation dashboard the growth team used.

To be upfront, I have about 1 year of experience, below the 2 listed.

My resume is attached. If this aligns with you, can we have a quick chat?

Regards,
Asha Rao
linkedin.com/in/asharao
